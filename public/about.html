About Us

House music was born in Chicago in the early 1980s. At the Warehouse club, DJ Frankie Knuckles blended disco, soul and European electronic records into long, continuous sets, and the music took its name from the club. Local DJs and producers began making their own tracks with affordable drum machines and synthesizers such as the Roland TR-808 and TR-909, built on a steady four-on-the-floor beat.

In 1984 Jesse Saunders released "On and On", often called the first house record. Chicago labels like Trax and DJ International followed with records from artists including Marshall Jefferson, Larry Heard and Phuture, whose "Acid Tracks" introduced the squelching Roland TB-303 sound that became acid house.

By the late 1980s house had crossed the Atlantic, powering the UK's Second Summer of Love and the rave scene across Europe. Since then it has branched into deep house, garage, tech house and many other styles, and it still fills dance floors around the world.
